Lesson 4.1 • Document Foundations Not yet completed

Format Documents So Readers Find Your Strengths Fast

A clear layout respects the reader’s time. When headings, spacing, margins, and bullet lists work together, an employer can spot your skills in seconds—and Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) can read your file accurately.

1. Watch me: Compare layout habits 2. Let’s do it & You try: Formatting Studio
Formatting Element Hard to Read Clear & Employer-Ready
Headings & Styles Same plain font size everywhere; sections blend together. Bold, slightly larger section headings (Summary, Skills, Experience, Education).
Line Spacing Cramped lines (1.0) that make paragraphs feel like a wall of text. Comfortable breathing room (1.15 to 1.5 line spacing) with space between sections.
Margins & Alignment Very narrow margins or centered body paragraphs with uneven edges. Balanced 0.75"–1.0" margins and consistent left alignment for easy scanning.
Bullet Lists Long run-on sentences packed with commas. Short bullet points starting with action words—one clear idea per line.

        Organize Folders, Name Files Clearly, and Keep Safe Backups

        Knowing where your files live prevents last-minute stress before an interview. Build one simple folder tree, give files predictable names, move old drafts to an archive folder, and save a second backup copy.

        1. Watch me: Naming & folder structure 2. You try: Clean up Jordan’s cluttered Downloads

        Clear File Naming Rule

        Use First_Last_DocType.ext with underscores or hyphens—never vague names like resume(2)FINAL.docx.

        Good: Jordan_Lee_Resume.docx and Jordan_Lee_Resume.pdf

        Folder Hierarchy & Version Control

        Keep active job files inside Documents / Job_Search / Applications. Move older versions to Archive with dates so you never attach the wrong draft.

        Local + Cloud Backup Habit

        Shared training computers may reset or sign out. Keep a second copy in your authorized cloud drive or personal USB drive—never rely on just one copy.

        Lesson 4.4 • Saving & Exporting Not yet completed

        Keep Both Versions: Editable (.docx) and Shareable (.pdf)

        Save your master work as an editable document (.docx) so you can update it later. When you apply for a job or send a résumé, export a fixed-layout .pdf and always reopen the PDF to check its layout before sending.

        1. Watch me: Compare .docx and .pdf 2. Let’s do it: Pick the right format 3. You try: Export & inspect a PDF
        Format Purpose When to Use It What to Watch For
        .docx (Editable) Revise text, add new skills, update dates for future roles. Whenever you are drafting, editing, or tailoring your résumé. Margins or fonts can shift slightly on another person’s computer.
        .pdf (Fixed-Layout) Preserves exact spacing, margins, and fonts across any device. Uploading to a job portal or attaching to a professional email. Always reopen and inspect the exported PDF for cut-off lines or wrong filenames.

        Responsible AI Bridge • Digital Literacy + AI Check Not yet completed

        Use AI as a Drafting Helper—Never as the Final Fact-Checker

        An AI assistant can help proofread grammar or turn rough notes into strong action verbs. However, AI often invents degrees, job titles, or numbers that are not true. You are always the author and decision-maker.

        1. Watch me: CLEAR prompting & safety 2. You try: Audit an AI draft for fabrications

        1. Protect Private Details

        Leave real names, SSNs, state IDs, phone numbers, home addresses, and personal history out of AI prompts. Use fictional or general placeholders instead.

        2. Reject AI Fabrications

        Never keep skills, certificates, or years of experience that AI added on its own. Compare every single AI sentence word-for-word against your real facts.

        3. Prompt with CLEAR

        Context • Let AI know the task • Explain details • Ask for format • Review & refine in your own words.
