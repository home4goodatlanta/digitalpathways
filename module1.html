Desktop & Icons

The desktop is your home screen. Just like the top of a real desk, it holds shortcuts to the folders and documents you use often.

Workplace habit: Keep only the folders you need on the desktop so it stays calm and easy to scan.

2. Let’s do it Four everyday mouse & trackpad actions (and keyboard alternatives).

1. Left-click (Select)

Tap once with your index finger (or press left button once) to select an icon or push a button. Keyboard: use Tab or arrows.

2. Right-click (Options)

Click the right side (or tap with two fingers on a trackpad) to open a menu of choices like Rename or Details. Keyboard: Shift + F10.

3. Double-click (Open)

Two quick taps on a desktop icon opens a file or folder. Keyboard: select the icon and press Enter.

4. Click-and-drag (Move)

Press and hold the left button while sliding to move a file into a folder, then release. Accessible option: use the Move button in the practice pad.

Stage 2 · Lesson 1.2

Keyboard, Typing & Essential Shortcuts

Not yet marked complete

Your goal: Type comfortably with uppercase and lowercase letters, fix typos cleanly using Backspace, Delete, and Arrow keys, and save time with Copy, Paste, Select All, and Undo.

Cursor Shift vs. Caps Lock Backspace vs. Delete Arrow Keys Copy & Paste Undo
1. Watch me Click any key group to see when to use it.

Shift vs. Caps Lock

Hold Shift while tapping a letter to make a single capital letter (like “J” in Jordan). Use Caps Lock only when you want ALL CAPS—tap it again to turn it off so emails don’t look like shouting.

2. Let’s do it
  1. Find the blinking line (the cursor): Click after a misspelled word or move there with the Arrow keys.
  2. Erase only what needs fixing: Press Backspace to erase to the left of the cursor, or Delete to erase to the right.
  3. Copy without retyping: Highlight a phrase, hold Ctrl and tap C to copy, click a new line, and hold Ctrl + V to paste.
  4. Step back anytime: If you delete something by accident, press Ctrl + Z right away to undo it!

Stage 3 · Lesson 1.3 & Accessibility

Managing Windows & Accessibility Controls

Not yet marked complete

Your goal: Switch smoothly between two open applications without losing your work, resize or minimize windows, and adjust text size and contrast for comfortable reading.

Minimize Maximize / Restore Close Alt+Tab / Task Switcher Display Zoom High Contrast
1. Watch me

− Minimize (Hide for now)

Tucks the window down onto the Taskbar without closing or losing your work. Click its name on the Taskbar to bring it right back.

□ Maximize / Restore

Expands a window to fill the entire screen so it is easier to read. Click it again to restore the smaller size.

× Close (Finished)

Shuts the window completely. Always save your document before clicking Close!

Zoom & High Contrast

Adjusting text size, screen zoom, or high-contrast colors is a standard workplace setting—not a sign of trouble. Use whatever makes reading clearest.

2. Let’s do it
  1. Drag a window by its top titlebar: Move it aside so both windows are visible on screen.
  2. Switch between apps: Click the other window, click its Taskbar tab, or use Switch Window (Alt+Tab).
  3. Practice Minimize and Maximize: Tuck a window to the taskbar and bring it back without losing text.
  4. Tune the display: Increase zoom to 125% or turn on High Contrast to see how much easier text is to scan.

Module 1 Exit Check & Answer Guide
1. What is an application?

Reference answer: Software used to perform a specific task (such as a text editor for writing or a web browser for reading pages).

2. Why should you save a document?

Reference answer: To preserve your work so you can find it, reopen it, and edit or share it later.

3. What should you do before leaving a shared workstation?

Reference answer: Lock or sign out according to site policy so your work stays private.
